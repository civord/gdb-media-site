Website Design for Carpenters — Australia-wide

Most of your work is referred. They still look you up first.

Someone passes on your number, and before they ring it they search your name. What they find decides whether the call happens at all. For a carpenter that search result is not lead generation — it is the referral either surviving or quietly dying.

Why It Matters

Carpentry covers a lot of ground, and that is the problem.

Your Range Confuses People

You might hang a door on Monday and build a deck on Thursday. A visitor who wants a deck needs to see decks in the first three seconds, not a list of eleven services they have to read through.

The Referral Check Is Real

Word of mouth still brings most carpentry work in, and almost all of it now passes through a search of your name first. No site, or a dead Facebook page, and the referral loses its confidence on the way to the phone.

Custom Work Needs Custom Quoting

You cannot price a built-in wardrobe from a contact form. What you can do is capture enough — the room, the span, a photo, a rough budget — that the first call is useful instead of twenty questions.

Service Pages That Rank

Every job you do, its own page.

Carpentry searches split hard by job type, and the money is very different at each end. Someone searching for a deck builder and someone searching for a door repair are not the same customer, so they should not land on the same page.

Getting Found Locally

Carpentry searches are tighter than you think.

Decking and pergola work is strongly seasonal and strongly local — people want someone who has built in their street, on their block size, under their council's rules. Website design for carpenters should name those places rather than claiming to cover all of Melbourne, which reads as a franchise and converts worse.

The other half is the referral search. Someone has your name and is checking you exist. If your Google Business Profile shows the right suburbs, recent photos and a handful of reviews, that check passes in seconds. If it shows nothing, the referral cools before the call.

Common Questions

Straight answers, no fine print.

How much does a carpentry website cost?

One fixed price, agreed before work starts and GST inclusive. A carpenter doing decks and pergolas needs a different site to one running fit-outs for builders, so the number follows the scope rather than an hourly rate.

Most of my work is word of mouth. Do I need a site at all?

More than you might think, and for a different reason. Referrals search your name before they call. The site is not there to find new customers so much as to stop the ones you have already earned from having second thoughts.

I do everything from small repairs to full decks. How do I show that?

By splitting it, not listing it. Each job type gets its own page and its own photos, so the person who wants a pergola sees pergolas immediately. A single services page covering everything reads as a catalogue and ranks for nothing.

Can you put my Instagram work on the site?

Yes, and for carpenters it is worth doing properly rather than as an embedded feed. Embeds load slowly and disappear if the account changes. We pull the best work onto the site itself where it loads fast and Google can read it.

What if I want to stop taking small jobs?

Then the site should stop advertising them. Pages are how you steer the enquiries you get — lead with decking and joinery, leave repairs off, and the mix of calls changes within a couple of months.
