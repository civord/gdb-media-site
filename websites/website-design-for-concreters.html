Website Design for Concreters — Australia-wide

Concreting is bought on finish. So show the finish.

Nobody can tell a good slab from a bad one in a quote email. They can tell instantly from a photo of a finished exposed-aggregate driveway in the sun. A concreter's website lives or dies on whether the work is visible — and most concreters have a camera roll full of it doing nothing.

Why It Matters

Three quotes, one photo gallery, one winner.

Price Is All They Can Compare

When three concreters quote a driveway and none of them show finished work, the homeowner has exactly one thing left to judge on. That is how you end up bidding against someone cutting the mesh out of the slab.

Finishes Need Showing, Not Describing

Exposed aggregate, honed, coloured, stamped, plain broom — the words mean nothing to a customer who has never bought concrete before. A photo of each, on a real job, does the explaining for you.

The Weather Runs Your Calendar

Concreting books in weather windows, so enquiries need to arrive steadily rather than in a rush. A site that pulls a trickle every week is worth more to you than one that pulls ten in a fortnight and nothing after.

Service Pages That Rank

Every job you do, its own page.

A homeowner does not search for a concreter. They search for the thing they want poured. Each of those is a different price, a different decision and a different page — which is how you rank for the jobs you actually want instead of the ones you don't.

Getting Found Locally

Concreting is a suburb-by-suburb trade.

Marketing for concreters comes down to being the visible option in a tight radius. Concrete is heavy, trucks have a window, and most jobs come from within thirty minutes of the yard. That makes a wide service area actively unhelpful — it dilutes the local signal without winning work you would take anyway.

Council crossover rules and permit requirements differ across Victorian councils, and homeowners search for exactly that. A page explaining the crossover process in the councils you work with picks up searches no competitor is bothering to answer, and marks you as the one who has done it before.

Common Questions

Straight answers, no fine print.

How much does a website cost for a concreting business?

One fixed number, quoted up front and GST inclusive, agreed before any work starts. A sole operator doing driveways needs a different site to a crew running slabs for builders, so there is no menu price. No hourly billing and no lock-in contract.

Can you build a gallery that separates the finishes?

Yes, and it is the single most useful thing on a concreter's site. Exposed aggregate, honed, coloured and plain each get their own set, so someone who wants exposed aggregate sees six examples of exposed aggregate rather than scrolling past slabs and footings.

What if my photos are just phone snaps?

Phone photos are fine and usually better than stock. What matters is the angle and the light — we will tell you exactly what to shoot and when, and most concreters already have years of it sitting on their phone.

Do you build separate pages for driveways and slabs?

Yes. Someone pricing a driveway and a builder pricing a slab are different customers searching different things. One page listing every service in dot points ranks for none of them.

Will it help me get commercial and builder work too?

It helps, though differently. Builders check you are licensed, insured and have done the volume before they call. That is a credentials page and a project list rather than a pretty gallery, and we build both.
