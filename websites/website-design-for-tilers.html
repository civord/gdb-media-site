Website Design for Tilers — Australia-wide

Websites for tilers that lead with the finish.

A customer chooses tiles at the showroom and thinks that is the decision. The thing that determines whether their bathroom is still sound in eight years is the waterproofing underneath, which they never see and rarely ask about. Say it out loud on your site and you are instantly a different kind of tiler.

Why It Matters

Sold on price, remembered for failure.

Waterproofing Is Your Real Product

It is certified, it is the expensive part to get wrong, and it is invisible. Most tiling websites do not mention it at all. Explaining it plainly turns a commodity quote into a conversation about who actually knows what they are doing.

Most Work Arrives Through Builders

Renovators and builders book tilers repeatedly, which makes a handful of trade relationships worth more than a hundred homeowner enquiries. That audience wants reliability, volume and clean sites — not a pretty mosaic gallery.

Large Format Changed The Job

Big slabs need different substrates, different tools and different skills, and plenty of tilers will not touch them. If you will, say so — it is a specific search with very little competition behind it.

Service Pages That Rank

Every job you do, its own page.

Tiling enquiries arrive attached to a room or a problem, almost never to the word tiling on its own. Each room and each problem searches differently, which is exactly why they each want their own page.

Getting Found Locally

Two audiences, two kinds of local.

Homeowner tiling searches are suburb-specific and renovation-driven — bathroom tiler plus a suburb, usually while a renovation is already underway. Being in the map pack for those areas is the whole game, and it is decided by proximity and reviews more than by the website.

Builder and renovator work is local in a different way. They want someone who can get to their sites across a region without travel blowing out the day rate. Stating the corridor you cover, rather than a list of suburbs, is what makes a tiler easy to add to a regular roster.

Common Questions

Straight answers, no fine print.

How much does a tiling website cost?

One fixed number, quoted before work starts and GST inclusive. A tiler working mainly for builders needs a very different site to one chasing homeowner bathroom jobs, so scope drives the price rather than an hourly rate.

Why should waterproofing be on the website?

Because it is the part that fails, the part that is certified, and the part almost no competitor talks about. A homeowner who has read one page explaining it stops comparing your quote to the cheapest one on price alone.

Most of my work comes from builders. Is a site still worth it?

Yes, and it should be built for them rather than for homeowners. Builders check capacity, insurance, the kind of jobs you have run and whether you finish on time. That is a credentials and project-list site, not a gallery of feature walls.

Can you show tiles by room instead of by product?

That is the right way to do it. Nobody searches for a tile size — they search for a bathroom, a splashback or a pool surround. Organising the gallery the way people shop makes the site easier to use and easier to rank.

Should I list the brands I work with?

Only where it helps you rank or reassures a trade customer. Brand names bring in some search traffic and signal you work at a certain level, but a wall of logos with no context just pushes your own work further down the page.
