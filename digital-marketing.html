Digital Marketing for Trades — Australia-wide

The work that starts after the site goes live.

A website is a shopfront. Digital marketing is everything that walks people past it. For a trade business that mostly comes down to two things: putting the work you have already done in front of people who are not searching yet, and paying for attention when you need it faster than SEO can deliver it.

The Three Levers

Slow and free, fast and paid, or the list you already have.

Organic: Social And Content

Before-and-after photos, a thirty-second clip from a job site, a profile that matches your website. Slow to compound and free to run, and still the most convincing proof you own. This is where most trades get the best return for the least money.

Paid: Ads When You Need Volume Now

Search ads put you at the top tomorrow instead of in six months. They also stop the day you stop paying, which makes them a tool rather than a strategy. Genuinely useful for a new business, a quiet month, or one service you want to push hard.

The Bit Everyone Forgets: Your Past Customers

People you have already done work for are the cheapest jobs you will ever win. A reminder that a service is due, or a note that you now do something you did not before, beats almost any ad spend — and costs close to nothing.

Where To Start

Most trades should start with one of these, not all three.

The honest answer for a sole trader with a new website is: get the site converting, get the Google Business Profile active, then start posting the work. Ads come later, when there is margin to spend and a site worth sending paid traffic to.

We would rather set you up on the one channel you will actually keep doing than sell you a package with four channels you will quietly abandon by the end of the month. Marketing that stops after six weeks is worse than marketing you never started, because you have paid for the setup and kept none of the compounding.

If you are not sure which lever applies to you, that is a five-minute conversation, not a proposal. Tell us what your last ten jobs came from and the answer is usually obvious.

Common Questions

Straight answers, no fine print.

What is the difference between this and SEO?

SEO gets you found by people who are already searching for your trade. Digital marketing puts you in front of people who were not searching yet. They solve different problems, and for most trades SEO is the one to get working first.

Do I need any of this if I am already busy?

Probably not right now, and we will say so. The time to start is when the pipeline is comfortable, not when it has dried up — because everything here takes weeks to build momentum. Starting marketing in a panic is how people end up paying for ads they cannot sustain.

How much should a tradie spend on marketing?

Less than most agencies will tell you. If you have a website that converts and a Google profile that is active, social media costs you time rather than money and will carry you a long way. Paid ads only make sense once those two are working and you want volume faster.

Can you just run all of it for me?

Some of it. Social content and posting, yes. Ad campaigns, case by case. What nobody can outsource is the raw material — the photos from your jobs and the honest detail about what you do. That part has to come from you.
